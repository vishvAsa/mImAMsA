## १
**Mīmāṃsā Darśana**  
**Dharma • Language • Action • Reality**

### What is Mīmāṃsā?
- Inquiry, investigation, critical examination
- Primarily: inquiry into the meaning of the Veda
- Pūrva-Mīmāṃsā: karma-kāṇḍa, dharma, injunction, ritual action
- Uttara-Mīmāṃsā: Vedānta, jñāna, Brahman

### Central Concern
- **dharma-jijñāsā** — inquiry into dharma
- “codanā-lakṣaṇo artha dharmaḥ”
- Dharma is known through Vedic injunction
- Not all duty is known through perception alone

### Authority of the Veda
- Veda is **apauruṣeya** — not authored by a human person
- It is a unique source for knowing dharma
- Its authority does not depend on an individual speaker

### Philosophy of Language
- **Śabda** is central
- How do words and sentences convey meaning?
  - **Abhihitānvayavāda** (Bhāṭṭa): words first convey individual meanings, then sentence meaning is formed
  - **Anvitābhidhānavāda** (Prābhākara): words convey connected meaning within the sentence

### Pramāṇas
| Pratyakṣa (perception) | Anumāna (inference) | Upamāna (comparison) |
|------------------------|---------------------|----------------------|
| Arthāpatti (postulation) | Śabda (verbal testimony) | Anupalabdhi (non-apprehension) |

### Knowledge and Validity
- **Svataḥ-prāmāṇya**: cognition is intrinsically valid
- Invalidity is known later through contradiction or defect

### Ontology
- **Reality** branches into:
  - Ātman (enduring self, knower, agent, enjoyer)
  - Manas (inner organ)
  - Śarīra & Indriya (body and senses)
  - Dravya, guṇa, karma (substance, quality, action)
  - Sāmānya / Jāti (universals)
  - Abhāva (absence)
  - Śakti (potency or capacity)
  - Apūrva (unseen potency linking action to future result)

### Karma and Duty
- **Nitya** — obligatory regular action
- **Naimittika** — occasional duty
- **Kāmya** — action for a desired result
- **Niṣiddha** — prohibited action
- **Prāyaścitta** — expiatory action
- Proper action produces consequences; apūrva connects act and result

### Vedic Sentence Types
- **Vidhi** — injunction
- **Niṣedha** — prohibition
- **Mantra**
- **Arthavāda** — praise / explanation / support
- **Nāmadheya** — naming passage

### Hermeneutic Tools
- Śruti, liṅga, vākya, prakaraṇa, sthāna, samakhyā
- Pradhāna and aṅga
- Krama (order)
- Atideśa, ūha, bādha

### Mokṣa
- Release from bondage, pain, and karmic entanglement
- Not centered on mystical absorption
- Rooted in right knowledge of duty and right action

### Major Schools
**A. Bhāṭṭa** (Kumārila Bhaṭṭa)
- Defends Veda strongly
- Accepts anupalabdhi distinctly
- Associated with abhihitānvaya

**B. Prābhākara** (Prabhākara Miśra)
- Associated with anvitābhidhāna
- Distinctive account of duty


### Relation to Other Darśanas
- Debates with Nyāya on validity, God, language, absence
- Shares realism with Vaiśeṣika
- Shares Vedic basis with Vedānta, but focuses on karma and dharma
- Critiques Buddhist views on self, language, and momentariness
- Strong engagement with Vyākaraṇa

### Why Mīmāṃsā Matters
- A science of dharma
- A discipline of interpretation
- A philosophy of meaningful action
- Relevant to law, ethics, language, ritual studies, education, and civilizational thought

## **Second Infographic: Mīmāṃsā Darśana — The Science of Meaningful Action**

### The Authority of Dharma
- **Dharma as Vedic Injunction**: Meaningful action prompted by authoritative Vedic statements, leading to desirable results.
- **The Veda is Apauruṣeya**: Scripture is authorless, beginningless, protected from human errors.
- **Svataḥ-Prāmāṇya (Self-Validity)**: All knowledge is inherently valid unless proven otherwise by later contradicting evidence.

### Dharma-jijñāsā (Inquiry into Duty)
Leads to **Meaningful Action**

### The Grammar of Duty
- **Nitya** (Obligatory)
- **Naimittika** (Occasional)
- **Kāmya** (Optional)

### Arthāpatti (Postulation)
A unique way of knowing by assuming facts necessary to reconcile conflicting observations.

### Vedic Sentence Categories
- **Vidhi** (Injunctions): Telling what **MUST** be done.
- **Niṣedha** (Prohibitions): Telling what **MUST NOT** be done.
- **Arthavāda** (Explanatory passages): That praise or support injunctions.

### The Mechanics of Action & Language
- **Action** → **Apūrva**: The Invisible Bridge → **Future Results**
- Apūrva = Unseen potency from action linking it to results.
